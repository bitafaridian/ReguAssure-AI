Research Question and Problem Definition

1. Research Background

Internal and external quality audits are important mechanisms for evaluating organizational processes, verifying compliance with defined requirements, identifying risks, and assessing the effectiveness of quality management systems.

In complex and highly regulated development environments, such as medical software development, audit activities involve reviewing documentation, evaluating evidence, assessing risks, verifying process conformity, and coordinating limited human and operational resources.

Both internal and external audits face challenges associated with audit planning, task prioritization, resource allocation, evidence analysis, execution time, and the increasing complexity of audit requirements.

Artificial Intelligence (AI) and mathematical optimization offer opportunities to support these processes through predictive risk assessment, effort estimation, intelligent scheduling, automated evidence analysis, and adaptive resource allocation.

However, internal and external auditing involve different organizational roles, objectives, responsibilities, and operational constraints. An effective optimization framework must therefore account for these differences while enabling a systematic approach to audit planning and execution.

2. Research Problem

The central research problem is the efficient planning, scheduling, and execution of internal and external quality audits under limited resources, uncertainty, and predefined quality requirements.

Audit planning and execution involve interdependent decisions concerning:

- Audit task prioritization based on risk and complexity.
- Estimation of audit effort and execution time.
- Allocation of qualified auditors and available resources.
- Scheduling of interdependent audit activities.
- Fulfillment of mandatory audit requirements.
- Evidence collection, verification, and traceability.
- Adaptation to unexpected findings and changes during execution.

Conventional planning approaches may not sufficiently integrate predictive risk assessment, resource allocation, execution feedback, and adaptive decision-making within a unified optimization framework.

The research investigates how AI and mathematical optimization can support these interconnected decisions while accounting for the operational differences between internal and external quality auditing.

3. Preliminary Research Gap

Existing research explores various aspects of audit optimization, including risk-based audit planning, resource allocation, audit scheduling, AI-supported auditing, and automated evidence analysis.

However, the extent to which predictive intelligence, uncertainty-aware optimization, agent-based execution, and feedback-driven adaptation can be integrated into a unified framework for internal and external quality auditing remains to be systematically investigated.

The preliminary research direction focuses on investigating an integrated framework that connects:

- Predictive estimation of audit risk, complexity, and effort.
- Multi-objective optimization of audit task selection, scheduling, and resource allocation.
- Agent-based execution of selected audit procedures.
- Dynamic re-optimization based on execution feedback.
- Human oversight, evidence traceability, and predefined audit quality requirements.

The research gap is preliminary and will be validated through structured literature analysis.

4. Main Research Question

How can an integrated, uncertainty-aware AI framework combining predictive intelligence, quality-constrained multi-objective optimization, and agent-based execution dynamically optimize the planning, scheduling, and execution of internal and external quality audits while maintaining audit quality, mandatory requirements, and evidence traceability?

5. Sub-Research Questions

Predictive Intelligence:

How can predictive AI estimate audit risk, task complexity, and execution effort, including associated uncertainty, to support the planning of internal and external quality audits?

 Multi-Objective Optimization:

How can a multi-objective optimization model prioritize, schedule, and allocate limited resources to audit activities while satisfying mandatory audit requirements, resource constraints, auditor qualifications, and predefined quality criteria?

Agent-Based Audit Execution:

How can AI agents coordinate and execute selected audit procedures through structured evidence retrieval, automated verification, and evidence-grounded documentation while preserving human oversight and audit traceability?

Dynamic Audit Adaptation:

How can execution feedback, unexpected findings, updated evidence, and changes in resource availability be incorporated into a dynamic mechanism for revising audit priorities and re-optimizing audit plans?

 Internal and External Audit Adaptability:

How can the proposed framework accommodate the distinct operational requirements, responsibilities, and constraints of internal and external quality audits while maintaining a consistent optimization methodology?

Experimental Evaluation:

To what extent does the integrated framework improve audit efficiency, resource utilization, risk-weighted coverage, and evidence quality compared with conventional and partially automated approaches?

6. Research Scope

The research focuses on AI-supported planning, scheduling, and execution optimization of internal and external quality audits.

The initial application context is Quality Management Systems in medical software development.

The research will investigate two audit contexts:

* Internal quality auditing: Audit activities conducted within an organization to evaluate internal processes, controls, documentation, risk management, and conformity with defined requirements.
* External quality auditing: Independent quality audit activities, initially focusing on supplier quality audits and external QMS assessments within the selected application context.

The proposed framework is intended to provide a configurable approach to audit process optimization rather than a framework limited to a single audit type.

The initial scope includes:

- Risk-based audit planning and prioritization.
- Predictive estimation of audit effort and uncertainty.
- Audit task scheduling and resource allocation.
- Task dependencies and operational constraints.
- Automated execution of selected audit procedures.
- Evidence completeness and traceability.
- Human review and approval.
- Dynamic re-optimization based on execution feedback.

The initial implementation and evaluation may use synthetic or appropriately authorized and anonymized data.

7. Research Objectives

The research aims to:

1. Develop predictive models for estimating audit risk, task complexity, and execution effort.
2. Formulate a quality-constrained multi-objective optimization model for audit planning, scheduling, and resource allocation.
3. Design an agent-based execution layer for selected evidence-related audit activities.
4. Develop a feedback-driven mechanism for dynamic audit plan adaptation.
5. Investigate the applicability of the framework to internal and external quality auditing.
6. Implement and experimentally evaluate the integrated framework.
7. Assess its performance, robustness, limitations, and potential generalizability.

8. Expected Research Outcomes

The expected research outcomes include:

- A formal mathematical model for audit planning, scheduling, and resource allocation.
- Predictive models for audit risk and execution effort.
- An integrated AI-based audit optimization prototype.
- An evidence-grounded agent execution workflow.
- A configurable framework for internal and external quality audit contexts.
- Experimental evaluation against conventional and partially automated baselines.
- A reproducible research framework and documented findings.

9. Research Status

Current phase: Research formulation and literature investigation.

The research questions, scope, and proposed contributions are preliminary. The final research gap, technical formulation, and methodology will be refined following a structured investigation of existing scientific literature
